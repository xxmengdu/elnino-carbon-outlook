The modeling framework

01 / HISTORY

ERA5

Historical meteorology establishes ecosystem conditions through early 2026.

02 / CLIMATE

ECMWF ensemble

51 forecast members explore July–December weather trajectories.

03 / FIRE

Fire predictions

Machine learning estimates fire occurrence and burned-area fraction.

04 / CARBON

Two vegetation models

ORCHIDEE-MICT and JULES simulate uptake, respiration and fire emissions.

The two models are run for each climate member, producing 102 simulations. Analysis uses a 0.5° grid and the June-initialized outlook for July–December 2026.

How is burned area predicted?

A random forest classifier predicts whether fire occurs. An LSTM regressor then estimates burned-area fraction. Separate models cover forest and non-forest land in 14 GFED regions.

Predictors combine climate and fire weather with population, land use and vegetation state.

View the fire-prediction workflow

What has been checked?

  • Climate forecasts: historical hindcasts and ERA5 overlap checks assess lead-time and regional errors.
  • Carbon models: comparisons with atmospheric inversions reproduce broad historical evolution, but underestimate cumulative sink weakening in earlier strong El Niño events.
  • Burned area: historical spatial comparisons have R² = 0.94 for multi-year mean BAF and 0.73–0.74 against independent products.

These R² values describe historical spatial agreement, not prospective 2026 forecast skill. The research also compares 2026 developments with available ERA5 and GFED5 near-real-time data.

Reference periods, uncertainty and model assumptions

Carbon and climate anomalies use corresponding 2016–2025 monthly means, excluding El Niño years. Selected cumulative historical NBP comparisons are detrended.

Burned-area references combine FireCCI51 observations for 2016–2020 with modeled BA for 2021–2025, excluding El Niño years. Relative BA anomaly is (BA − reference BA) / reference BA.

Climate-member spread and vegetation-model differences describe separate uncertainties. The reported ± values are not automatically 95% confidence intervals; agreement on the sign of a change is not a calibrated impact probability.

DGVM land use is fixed at 2010. Fire-model land cover is fixed at 2022 for 2021–2026; 2026 population uses 2025 values. The future branches share non-meteorological forcing assumptions.

The study reports that July–August tropical ERA5 conditions were hotter and drier than the forecast ensemble, which may lead to some carbon losses being underestimated. This does not establish the full-period observed carbon balance.
