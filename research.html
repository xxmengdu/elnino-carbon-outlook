About the carbon outlook

  1. Home
  2. About the carbon outlook

NASA / ISS · 16 Jan 2026 · Use terms

Anticipating carbon-cycle risks while the event is still unfolding.

Why this outlook?

El Niño can reshape heat, rainfall and fire conditions far beyond the tropical Pacific. Those changes influence how much carbon ecosystems absorb and release.

Carbon-cycle impacts are often assessed after an event. This outlook asks where and when risks may emerge during the developing 2026 El Niño, before the full consequences are known.

What does it cover?

The outlook focuses on land carbon sinks and fire in July–December 2026. It combines an ECMWF seasonal climate ensemble, two vegetation models and machine-learning fire predictions.

Regional timing and uncertainty matter: gains in one region can partly offset severe losses elsewhere.

How is the outlook made?

Seasonal weather trajectories are translated into ecosystem responses and predicted burned area. The result is a geographically explicit outlook for carbon-sink weakening and fire exposure.

The research collaboration

The outlook is led at Tsinghua University, with collaborators at LSCE / Université Paris-Saclay, the University of Exeter. The full author list and institutional details are available in the research paper.
