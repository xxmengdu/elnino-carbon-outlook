FAQs about El Niño and carbon

NASA / ISS · 16 Jan 2026 · Use terms

A guide to the event, the carbon cycle and the outlook.

What is El Niño?

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, involving coupled changes in the tropical Pacific Ocean and atmosphere. Its effects on temperature, rainfall and ecosystems vary across regions.

What does “super El Niño” mean?

“Super” is widely used in public reporting. WMO notes that it is not a standardized operational category; agency assessments use terms such as “very strong.” The site retains the terminology and date of each original source. See agency updates and reporting.

What is a land carbon sink?

A land carbon sink absorbs more carbon than it releases. Vegetation takes up carbon through photosynthesis, while respiration, fire and other processes return carbon to the atmosphere.

What does a negative NBP anomaly mean?

It means less net uptake, or greater release, than in the reference period. It does not by itself show that the land has become an absolute net carbon source.

Why can regional impacts be severe when the global change is smaller?

Losses in one region can be partly offset by increased uptake elsewhere. The regional outlook therefore highlights both location and timing.

Is this a continuously updated forecast?

This site presents one event-focused research forecast initialized on 1 June 2026 for July–December 2026. Later agency reports provide dated background; they do not update the simulation ensemble.

How certain are the results?

The 51 climate members and two vegetation models give a range of possible responses. Their spread does not sample every source of uncertainty. Sign agreement is not a calibrated probability, and ± values should be read with the original figure definitions.

Why are peatlands important?

Peatlands hold large accumulated carbon stocks. Drying, drainage and ignition can make them vulnerable to persistent fires. The estimated 12.18 million hectares refers to risk-exposed peatland area, not hectares predicted to burn.

See How the outlook is made, The Data Hub and Figures and graphs. The research paper and supplement provide the full technical account.
