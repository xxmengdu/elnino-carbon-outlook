NASA / ISS · 16 Jan 2026 · Use terms

A modest global change can conceal severe regional losses.

A weaker global land carbon sink

The projected global cumulative NBP anomaly is −0.23 ± 0.21 Pg C for July–December. Increased respiration and fire emissions outweigh part of the gain in photosynthesis. This describes a change from the reference period, rather than the absolute land carbon balance.

Carbon-sink weakening in eastern South America

Tropical South America has a projected NBP reduction of 0.24 ± 0.10 Pg C; 44 of the 51 climate members project a negative anomaly. Hotspots extend across northeastern and central Brazil. Southern Africa also contains areas of projected sink weakening.

Across 11.4% of global land, and 16.4% of tropical land, projected negative NBP anomalies are more severe than in both 2015 and 2023 under the study’s 5% threshold.

Fire risks overlap carbon-rich peatlands

Equatorial Asia stands out for projected burned-area increases. About 12.18 million hectares of tropical forested peatlands overlap elevated fire risk. This is exposed peatland area, not predicted burned area.

Protecting peatland hydrology and reducing ignition pressure are priorities highlighted by the research.
